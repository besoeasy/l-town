# L-TOWN — Lore

## Overview

In 3049, humans never touch the ground. Robots go down. Operators stay in orbit. L-Town is a broadcast trial where open amateurs pilot RX-11 swarms for fame, sponsors, and a shot at the off-world circuit — army, mining, and general labor.

## Year 3049 — The Remote Age

After the Helios Storms of 2910 — solar flares that stripped surface shielding — planetary surfaces turned hostile to unshielded humans. The burn is slow, hours to days, not instant. No treaty bans landings. It is simply not worth the cost: sending a human takes years, while an operator can link instantly to a chassis already printed on that planet or held on a starship nearby.

Humans no longer fight physically. No boots on the ground, no bodies on other planets — our bodies cannot survive their environments. Exploration, mining, hostile clearance, and war are done by robots. Pilots connect from stations above.

## The RX-11 Chassis

Every unit in L-Town is the same RX-11 chassis: an inert nanobot shell held in humanoid form. Without a core it has no energy and does nothing. Same hull. Same speed. Same wiring. The swarm could reshape, but the link cannot — a neural hard limit, not a rule. Non-humanoid signals come back unreadable; pilots who try lag out or worse. Operators think in arms and legs, so the signal locks the mass to a body pilots can feel.

No variants. No upgrades. Fixed by arena charter so only cores compete. A swarm is cheap to print and easy to ship — built for worlds humans can never touch. One chassis serves as army, miner, and general robot.

A destroyed swarm is reclaimed on the spot and reprinted. Dawn prints start full — rushed reprints boot at 75% hull (375). Full strength takes time the trial does not give. A disconnected operator wakes up.

## Hull — Nanite Mass

Hull is a census. The number is how many nanites remain in the swarm.

Nanites are fuel, function, and mass at once. Fire spends them — every projectile is nanites leaving the body. High jumps burn crowds of them for lift. Shield is a nanite covering that absorbs damage. Super overclocks the burn — conduits flare golden-amber, visible across the map. The rust colors themselves are trivia only engineers care about; no sect rites them.

Nanites are made by the core, not the body. The core rusts them onto its own outer layer like iron in open air — fresh amber cooling through red to black — and output is core-limited: outside air density barely matters. When the robot moves and fights, the core spends its output as energy. When the robot stands still, draw drops and the core shifts to fabrication — that is the recharge. Crouched, the core supplies no external energy at all, so it rebuilds fastest. As hull runs low the pyramid tips starve evenly — the whole body weakens together, no limbs lost. Nothing is free — the body is the ammunition. Exceptions steal instead of build: Mednix recycles waste heat into mass, Surge and Parasite rip nanites off nearby swarms. When a link severs, the destroyed chassis expels a loose nanite cache (+100 Hull) onto the soil before printers reclaim the wreckage. Any nearby shell that moves within 5u siphons the mass — rightful salvage, and the crowd cheers the scramble.

## The Atma Core

Each shell is powered by an Atma Core. Atma means soul. The core is a perfect pyramid suspended center-mass in nanite fluid — a floating heart, no hard mount — its tips wired as connectors to the limbs. Insert the core and it takes the whole robot — directs every nanite, and the body takes the core's form. The core is the identity.

Standard cores still serve off-world as army, miner, and general labor issue. The trials are a customs showcase: this season only the eleven Maker customs are legal — no vanilla. Each holds a copied mind-pattern of its maker's founder — a transit engineer, a cult ghost, a pit-fighter. Mixed ledger: some gave, some were taken, and the glitch severity shows which. Copies glitch. The glitch is the ability. Submissions open every year — any Maker can file a new glitch next season.

Swap the core, change the soul. The body stays the same.

## The Makers

Eleven organisations submitted custom cores for this season. Fizzle rule: no valid target means the glitch fires empty — full cooldown, no refund:

- **Telepotu** — Vela Relay Compact. Breakaway freight engineers, ex-Meridian. Warp logistics. Escape tool for cave-ins and ambushes — swaps with a random enemy within 120 units.
- **Chumantr** — Pale Choir stealth cult. Survey sneak for hostile ground. Full ghost for 10 seconds — move only, no shoot, no Q/R/Super. The trials fund a hidden assassin army.
- **Denja** — Kuro Racer Syndicate. Overdrive engines, Atma governor caps burst at 8s. 2× speed, free, no frailty.
- **Mednix** — Helix Med-Corps. Field repair, self only. Pure random 1–50 hull instantly, capped at full.
- **Tank** — Bastion Siege Foundry. Bulwark doctrine. Takes half damage for 8 seconds, full speed.
- **Anchor** — Orbital Guard. Aegis shields. Pre-charged cell fires one 3s immunity, reusable every 40s, zero hull cost.
- **Surge** — Deep Vein Mining Guild. Siphon tech. Rips 30 hull off the nearest enemy within 40 units, keeps 15 — rest lost to heat.
- **Jinx** — Black Lotus AI Lab, feared vendor: everyone buys, nobody turns their back. Retaliation code. Dying swarm fires a dead-man overload into its killer within 60 units (80 hull) — snipers safe, no killer means no curse.
- **Gambler** — Vesper Casino-State. Desperate odds, equal thirds: heal +200 (capped), teleport onto enemy, or instant death. Volunteers are cheap because trial death is only a disconnect — the chassis rebuilds and they requeue.
- **Parasite** — Green Hive, a bio-corp selling leech tech. Leech ecology with kin filter — other Parasites are kin and immune. Drains 8/s off all strangers within 15 units for 6 seconds, keeps half.
- **Berserker** — Red Pit Fighters. Pit-raised stock fighting its way up. Chemical overclock for nanites, pilot stays sober. +50% damage and +25% speed for 8 seconds, then burnout crash −50 hull.

## L-Town Protocol

L-Town — L for Link, the first remote-link port, the name stuck — is a decommissioned transit hub kept as a battleground for testing new tech — sacred ground, the soil where the first chassis was ever printed and linked from orbit, marked by stones, no relic. Nobody lives down there: only shells and drones touch the soil. Its roads, garage, and office tower remain from the Meridian freight days — the tower a gutted link-control ruin, cover but no rooms. Meridian keeps the printers and walls as neutral host and fields no core — its own camera drones fly every unit, open footage for all, and neutral refs keep the contest honest: rigging rumored, never proven, logs always clean. Vela guests get no favors: Meridian is polite on camera and bitter behind it. The techs themselves are an invisible crew, never named on air. Two centuries of clean dawns — the print has never failed.

Makers prove their cores here. Winning pilots sign together with their winning core as a package — army clearance pays most and every pit kid dreams of it, with mining and general labor behind. Combat in the arena is the job interview. The marked top pilot is no accident: buyers pay to watch the leader under pressure.

Each trial runs 10 minutes to fit the relay programming block. Highest score wins, frags only, latest kill breaks ties — the timestamped kill clock settles it live on air. Cheap expendable drone swarms track every unit for the live leaderboard — losses expected. The leader runs ringed in a visible drone halo, in-sky for all to hunt — but frags are frags, no bounty, the halo is just light. Trials run dawn to dusk with instant requeue between sweeps; the card never pauses, no holidays, no anthem — crowd noise only. Top 3 sign with names only, no speeches, then sealed orders: pilots learn the soil when they land, families get censored letters through the mesh, and washed contractors come home to call the show. The booth is ex-pilots on one-hot-year cycles — ratings fade, the crowd moves on. The mesh auto-dubs the polyglot booth clean; nobody notices. Feuds are invented weekly and the books line everything: first kill, HVT survival, storm calls. Sponsors fund the endless reprints and top up the scattered mass daily — in return the cores run their liveries and the trials carry their names. Storm-night specials change nothing but the light: Meridian techs read the sky, lamps go up, same rules, pure spectacle. Helios itself is myth now — tall tales, nobody agrees, no canon version. Every dawn prints blank: tags wiped, one fair town. Shrines at the marked soil stand protected under heritage law — the printers route around them.

The arena regenerates each dawn. One map per day — everyone fights the same town for fair comparison, daily seed only, no private layouts. Printers recycle the wreckage overnight and print a fresh layout by morning.

## Operators

Operators are open amateurs on scrap-built couches — quality varies wildly, but link is link and rich pilots glide no smoother than poor ones. Anyone whose rig fits can join from home or ship, kids included — no license, no team, no age gate. The link suppresses hunger; grinders fast through the card and eat between slots. Amateurs are cheap and expendable twin engines: thousands of free crash-test pilots feeding Maker data, and the crowd loves watching itself. Losers link tomorrow — fame is a numbers game — and white-out stories are the common currency. Most fight for viral fame and leaderboard clips on the ownerless mesh of linked rigs. Callsigns go uncensored — the crowd sorts it out.

Death in L-Town is a disconnect, not a death: a white-out slam, headache and disorientation, no treatment — amateurs shake it off, part of the game. Dropped links kneel the shell for 15 seconds, core holding its breath, then the pilot is gone or back. Offline, venue-owned practice shells drill the solo hopefuls on retired champ patterns — champ and venue split the fee. Fringe sects keep small shrines at the old port and mainstream laughs. The best amateurs get signed to serve off-world as army, miners, and general robots — through the same swarms.
